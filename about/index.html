Our story

Six years of alpaca.

From a side project to a plug-in that journals, theses and conference papers use and cite.

2020

The first alpaca

Alpaca4d starts as a Grasshopper plug-in that drives OpenSees without a line of Tcl. The first commit lands in April.

2021

Growing a community

The plug-in is shared with the Grasshopper community, new contributors send code, and supporters on Patreon help keep it going.

2022

Into research

Alpaca4d appears in a PhD thesis at the University of Catania on structural models of whole city blocks, one of the first of the publications that now use or cite it.

2023

Hello, Mac

Alpaca4d runs on macOS too, and installs straight from Rhino's Package Manager.

2024

Shells and domes

Papers from IWSS 2023 use it to study Heinz Isler's shells, geodesic domes and hyperbolic paraboloid shells.

2026

A new chapter

A commercial release with a free tier for small models, and a busy year: non-linear and topology optimisation, ten-node tetrahedra, linear buckling and the Explorer web app.

What we believe

Four rules we build by.

01

OpenSees for everyone

OpenSees is one of the most capable structural solvers there is, and it is open source. Designers should be able to use it without learning Tcl first.

02

Your models stay yours

Alpaca4d writes plain, documented OpenSees files. Keep them, edit them, archive them, or run them again without Alpaca4d at all.

03

Details matter

Every component documented with a picture, every colour checked for contrast, every element checked against a benchmark.

04

Trust, but verify

Results should always be checked against a second finite element package. We say so up front, and we publish our benchmarks.
