# Isler Shell: LinkedIn post

A three-point shell 24 m a side, after Isler, form-found by Kangaroo and analysed with Alpaca4d under its
own weight, with its principal stress lines in its surface. Here: the form being found, analysed at
every step (`isler-shell-form-finding.mp4`), the lines growing (`isler-shell-growing.mp4`), the shell
spinning (`isler-shell-spin.mp4`) and walked under (`isler-shell-walk-through.mp4`), each with a `.gif`;
from above, below and outside (`isler-shell.png`, `-clean.png`, `-top.png`, `-top-clean.png`,
`-below.png`, `-outside.png`), the canvas (`grasshopper-definition.png`) and the canvas beside the
result (`isler-shell-in-grasshopper.png`).

## The numbers, if someone asks

- **The form:** a triangular net 24 m a side, pinned at its three corners, pushed up by Kangaroo, its
  edges keeping their length.
- **The shell:** concrete, 100 mm thick, under its own weight; membrane lines, 0.5 m apart.
- **The video:** Kangaroo's iterations moved evenly from the flat net to the finished form; at each, the
  model is analysed and its lines traced again.
- **Needs:** Kangaroo2, which Rhino 7 and 8 ship; a licence, as the shell is over the free 50 elements.
